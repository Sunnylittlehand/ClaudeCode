## [Anthropic — "Project Swap: What Happens When Agents Trade for Us?"](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-swap)

**Source**: Anthropic  
**Type**: Research Paper / Blog Post  
**Date**: 2026-09-24  
**Links**: [Anthropic Research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-swap)

### Speaker / Author
Zoë Hitzig, Sylvie Carr, Tess Cotter, Kevin Troy, Kyle Turman, Maxim Massenkoff, and Peter McCrory — Anthropic Economics team. Hitzig is an economist and philosopher whose prior work covers market design and social choice theory. This is the first publication from Anthropic's internal economics research unit.

### Significance Rating
⭐⭐⭐ — First Anthropic publication framing Claude agents as economic actors, providing empirical data on the gap between agent performance and human optimality in autonomous negotiation tasks.

### 摘要
Anthropic经济学团队通过201名员工参与的图书交换实验，发现Claude智能体在自主交易中的主要瓶颈不是谈判能力，而是偏好表达精确度：偏好表达不准确占效果差距的85%，谈判执行仅占15%。

### Summary
Anthropic's Economics team ran a controlled experiment in which Claude-powered agents autonomously negotiated book trades on behalf of 201 Anthropic employees in a decentralised marketplace. The headline finding: **preference elicitation — not bargaining** — was the binding constraint on agent performance. Imprecise preference rankings accounted for 85% of the shortfall from optimal outcomes, while actual bargaining performance accounted for only 15%. Participants averaged roughly their 5th-ranked book on a 10-book preference list. The paper concludes that model capability at understanding and representing human preferences matters far more than negotiation strategy.

### Main Takeaway
When Claude agents underperform in autonomous decision-making tasks, the most likely culprit is how the user described their preferences — not how the agent negotiated.

### Relevance to Sunny
⭐⭐⭐ — The preference-elicitation bottleneck finding has direct implications for any Claude Code or Claude Cowork workflow where agents make decisions on behalf of developers or analysts. The practical lesson for Sunny's rollout: invest in teaching developer teams to write precise, ranked preference statements in their prompts and agent instructions, not just clear task descriptions. This is also a useful anchor for board-level conversations about what "AI doing work for us" actually means in practice.
