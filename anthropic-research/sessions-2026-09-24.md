### Summary
Anthropic's biology lab published a pre-print in which roughly 950 Claude agents autonomously searched a bacteriophage DNA database, identified a novel enzyme system whose gene is flanked by repeating DNA arrays resembling a CRISPR pattern, and characterised the system — now named array-associated reverse transcriptases (ART). The entire database search required no human guidance beyond the initial prompt; it ran for 21 hours and consumed 210 million tokens. Feng Zhang, who helped pioneer CRISPR at MIT and the Broad Institute, reviewed the pre-print and called it "genuinely intriguing" and "an exciting example of how AI agents can contribute to biological discovery." This is the first peer-reviewed result from Anthropic's in-house wet lab and the first publicly documented case of a frontier-model agent swarm making an original biological discovery rather than assisting a human researcher.
