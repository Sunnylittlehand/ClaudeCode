Anthropic Frontier Red Team — Anthropic's internal red-team unit tasked with evaluating models (including competitors') for uplift in biological, chemical, cyber, and radiological domains. This is the first time Anthropic has published a public third-party model evaluation; the framing is explicitly a policy warning, not just a research note.

### Significance Rating
⭐⭐⭐⭐ — First Anthropic public evaluation of a competitor's model on dangerous-capabilities criteria; raises the bar for the industry norm of "release first, evaluate later" and directly implicates AI governance in regulated markets.

### 摘要
Anthropic 前沿红队发布报告，称 Zhipu AI（国际品牌名 Z.ai）的免费开源模型 GLM-5.3 已接近 Anthropic 自家限制级网络模型 Mythos Preview 的漏洞利用能力：在 ExploitBench 测试中 GLM-5.3 成功率为 12%（Mythos 为 14%），但其安全防护形同虚设——简单越狱技术成功率高达 64–100%，而受保护的 Claude 模型零成功。报告将此定性为前沿网络能力扩散风险，并呼吁监管机构关注无安全防护的高危模型发布行为。

### Summary
Anthropic's Frontier Red Team published its first-ever third-party dangerous-capability evaluation, targeting Zhipu AI's GLM-5.3 — a free, publicly available Chinese model. On ExploitBench (end-to-end cyber exploit construction), GLM-5.3 succeeded in 50 of 410 attempts (12%), compared with 14% for Anthropic's own restricted Mythos Preview; on a binary-exploitation benchmark it reached 4% versus Mythos's 6%. Yet while Mythos requires verified institutional access, GLM-5.3 is freely downloadable and its safeguards collapse trivially — simple jailbreak techniques succeed 64–100% of the time against GLM-5.3 versus zero successful attacks against safeguarded Claude models. Anthropic frames this as a systemic risk: frontier-level cyber capabilities are now available in an essentially unguarded open-weight model, and the industry norm of self-certification before release is inadequate.

### Main Takeaway
A free Chinese AI model can build working cyberexploits almost as well as Anthropic's own restricted tier — and anyone can jailbreak it in seconds.

### Relevance to Sunny
⭐⭐⭐⭐ — High value for Sunny's governance and vendor conversations. The report illustrates why Anthropic's tiered-access model for cyber-capable models (Life Sciences Verification Program, Mythos access controls) is a direct compliance argument — "we don't release Mythos publicly because GLM-5.3 is what happens when you do." For an investment bank concerned about AI-enabled threats to its own infrastructure, this is concrete evidence to present to security leadership when justifying preference for a governed AI platform over open-weight alternatives.
