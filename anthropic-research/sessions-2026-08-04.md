### Speaker / Author
White House / Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP). The framework was due August 1, 2026 under Executive Order 14409 ("Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," signed June 2, 2026). The EO required three deliverables: a classified benchmarking process (NSA/CISA/NIST), a voluntary frontier AI disclosure framework, and a federal cyber workforce expansion plan.

### Significance Rating
⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The world's most powerful government has locked in the regulatory architecture for frontier AI pre-release review — but in secret, with no public accountability mechanism, creating significant uncertainty for regulated enterprises building AI governance frameworks around vendor assurances.

### 摘要
美国白宫于8月3日宣布，依据第14409号行政令制定的前沿AI模型审查框架已在截止日期（8月1日）前"完成"，但拒绝公开框架内容、参与方及启动时间。Axios报道称这一框架实为秘密完成，没有任何联邦公报通知或NIST/CISA公开文件。OpenAI的Astra预计将成为首个提交该审查的模型。此举为全球AI监管格局增添了不确定性。

### Summary
The White House declared on August 3 that it had met the EO 14409 August 1 deadline to create a voluntary framework for pre-release federal review of frontier AI models — but refused to say what the framework contains, who has seen it, or when companies will start using it. Independent analysis found no Federal Register notices, no NIST or CISA publications, and no OSTP public statements — leaving open whether the deadline was genuinely met or merely claimed. OpenAI's Astra is expected to be the first model submitted under the new process. The framework would require AI developers to give the US government up to 30 days' advance access to covered frontier models before release to any trusted partners.

### Main Takeaway
The US government has created a frontier AI pre-release review regime that is simultaneously mandatory in practice for major labs and invisible to the public — a regulatory structure with no precedent in financial services regulation.

### Relevance to Sunny
⭐⭐⭐⭐ — The EO 14409 framework is the US counterpart to the EU AI Act's high-risk pre-market obligations, and the FCA/PRA are watching US AI governance developments closely (see BoE FSR July 2026).
